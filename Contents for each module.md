## Module 1: What is a sustainable diet

## Learning Objectives:

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:

1. Define a sustainable diet and explain its key components in relation to health and environmental sustainability.

2. Identify at least four core sustainable diet principles.

Core Content: https://youtu.be/T7RFNuHIUhE?si=oLSd80fiJacwg013

Supplements: sustainable diet principles infographic

## Action Steps:

1. Spot the principles in your own meals (Think about one meal you ate in the past 24 hours. Tick which sustainable diet principles it already follows (e.g., plant-based foods, moderate meat intake, minimal food waste, seasonal or local foods).

2. Principle recognition check (From the list of sustainable diet principles introduced in this module, choose four that you think are most relevant to your current lifestyle and campus food environment.)

3. One-principle focus for the week (Select one sustainable diet principle you would like to pay more attention to over the coming week (e.g., adding more vegetables, reducing food waste, choosing local foods when possible).

4. Reflection prompt (optional, low-pressure) (In one sentence, write down why this principle matters for your health or the environment.)

## Module 2: Our Choices Matter

## Learning Objectives:

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:

1. Explain how food is linked to big environmental issues.

2. Describe the benefits of healthy food for you and for the planet.

3. Explain how your food choices can have an impact on the planet and other people.

### Core Contents: https://youtu.be/EyvgpqEGjcU

Supplements: image on environmental impact

## Action Steps:

1. Connect food to environmental issues (Review the examples presented in this module (e.g., food waste, packaging, meat production). Match one food-related practice to one environmental issue (e.g., food waste → greenhouse gas emissions).

2. Health–planet benefit pairing (Choose one healthy food choice discussed in the module (e.g., fruits and vegetables, whole grains, reduced sugary drinks) and list one benefit for your health and one benefit for the environment.)

3. Personal impact reflection (Think about one food choice you commonly make on campus (e.g., takeaway meals, packaged snacks, sugary drinks). Indicate one way this choice may affect the environment or other people (e.g., waste generation, resource use, workers).

4. Key takeaway identification (Write down one key message from this module that changed how you think about the relationship between food, health, and the environment).

## Module 3: Buying Food Locally

## Learning Objectives:

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:

1. Discuss the environmental and social impact of foods depending on where and how it was produced and transported.

2. Compare the environmental footprint of food products depending on origin and seasonality.

3. Prioritize actions to reduce the negative impact of the foods you eat

### Core Content: https://youtu.be/qq-QkVbUEVM

Supplement: 9 Inexpensive Superfoods You Might Find in Your Malaysian Diet, and food carbon footprint table https://story.motherhood.com.my/blog/9-inexpensive-superfoods-you-might-find-in-your-malaysian-diet/

## Action Steps:

1. Production and transport awareness (Select one commonly consumed food item (e.g., chicken rice, apples, bottled beverages). Identify where it is produced and how it is transported (local vs imported; fresh vs processed)

2. Footprint comparison (Compare two similar food products (e.g., local seasonal fruit vs imported fruit; locally prepared meal vs packaged food). Indicate which option likely has a lower environmental footprint

3. Impact-reduction prioritisation (From the strategies discussed in this module (e.g., choosing seasonal foods, reducing packaging, buying locally when possible), select one action you could prioritise in your usual food choices to reduce negative environmental impact.)

4. Feasibility check (optional but strong) (Identify one barrier (e.g., cost, time, availability) and one possible solution to applying your chosen action in your daily routine.)

## Module 4: Reading Labels

## Learning Objectives:

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:

1. Identify information on food labels that is important to you.

2. Make informed choices when shopping for food that is good for your health and the planet.

3. Construct nutritious and well-balanced meals.

Core Content: https://youtu.be/7KW57Vo3WWE

Supplement: label information https://unccelearn.org/course/view.php?id=56&sectionid=702

## Action Steps:

1. Label scanning and prioritisation (When purchasing a packaged food item, identify three pieces of label information that matter most to you (e.g., sugar, sodium, fat content, ingredients list, country of origin, packaging type).

2. Health—environment trade-off check (Compare two similar food products (e.g., breakfast cereals, snacks, beverages) using the nutrition label and packaging information. Choose the option that is healthier and/or more environmentally friendly)

3. Real-life application (Identify one small change you can apply the next time you shop or eat out (e.g., checking sodium content, choosing less packaging, adding one extra vegetable dish).

## Module 5: Malaysian Healthy Plate

## Learning Objectives:

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:

1. Describe the key components and proportions of the Malaysian Healthy Plate.

2. Apply the Malaysian Healthy Plate model to construct nutritionally balanced meals in everyday eating contexts, including home meals and campus dining.

Core Content: https://youtu.be/D8FQqpD_Fjw?si=Rncxw4FUXbWcO1jU

Supplement: https://imu.edu.my/imunews/eating-healthy-with-the-malaysian-healthy-plate

## Action Steps:

1. Plate recognition and understanding (Using a provided image of the Malaysian Healthy Plate, identify the recommended proportion of vegetables and fruits, protein foods, and staple foods. Match each food group with at least one common Malaysian food example (e.g., nasi, ikan, tempeh, sayur).

2. Meal assessment using MHP (Think about a recent meal you ate (e.g., nasi lemak, economy rice, mee goreng). Compare it with the Malaysian Healthy Plate model and identify one component that is missing or overrepresented.)

3. Plate reconstruction (Redesign the same meal to better align with the Malaysian Healthy Plate by making at least one realistic change (e.g., adding vegetables, reducing portion size of rice, choosing grilled instead of fried protein).

4. Real-life commitment (Set one specific goal to apply the Malaysian Healthy Plate at your next meal or when eating out on campus (e.g., “I will fill half my plate with vegetables at lunch tomorrow”).

## Module 9: Food Waste & Packaging

Learning Objectives:

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:

1. Discuss how food waste and packaging affects nature, societies and economies.

2. Distinguish instances when food packaging is necessary and when it can be avoided.

3. Identify strategies to prevent, reduce, or recycle food waste and packaging.

Core Content: https://youtu.be/k-m0LDbkVhg

Action Steps:

1. Buy only what you can eat. Plan your meals and be mindful of expiration dates.

2. Separate your waste and recycle as much as possible. Compost food you throw away.

3. Try to buy foods that are unpackaged whenever possible. Bring along re-usable items you need in your everyday life, such as shopping bag, bottle, coffee mug, and food container.
